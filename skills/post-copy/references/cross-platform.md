# Cross-platform adaptation

The same asset, different text habits. The numbers are in
`references/kb-visual-formats.md` §2.2 to §2.5.

## Instagram: the carousel as PNG slides

- Upload the PNGs from `slides/`. They are 1080x1350; the profile grid shows
  them cropped to 3:4, and the layouts keep everything essential clear of the
  outer 40px.
- Up to 20 slides.
- A shorter caption than LinkedIn's: the hook in the first line, the question at
  the end.
- A few specific hashtags at the end. No dataset in the reference settles a
  number; staying under five avoids looking automated.
- Instagram can show slide 2 first to someone who scrolled past the cover, so
  slide 2 has to stand on its own.

## X: a thread

- Text leads on X (§2.3). Turn the deck into a thread: the first post is the
  cover's hook, then one post per evidence slide, then the question.
- At most four images per post. Attach the slides that are evidence (code, a
  metric, a diagram), one per post; a portrait slide shows best alone.
- Each post within 280 characters, unless the user has Premium and wants a
  single long post.

## Threads: the carousel with a short post

- A post holds 500 characters, and a carousel takes up to 20 items.
- Photos outperform text-only posts there (§2.4), so post the slides.
- For a long version, use a text attachment (up to 10,000 characters) instead
  of screenshots of text, which screen readers cannot read.
- Engagement on Threads comes from replying to comments.

## Never

- Paste the LinkedIn caption, hashtags included, unchanged everywhere.
- Post a screenshot of text without alt text.
- Suggest that the system publishes on these platforms. It does not.
